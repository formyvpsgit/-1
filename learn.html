The 50 / 30 / 20 rule

The simplest way to split your salary before you spend a single dirham:

50%Essentials
30%Choices
20%Savings
  • Essentials: housing, bills, food, insurance, loan installments.
  • Choices: dining out, shopping, subscriptions, entertainment.
  • Savings: emergency fund Ffirst, then investing — move it automatically on payday.
Core concepts

Six words to know before any investment

Emergency fund

An amount covering 6 months of your commitments in an easy-to-withdraw account. Build it before any investing — it's what protects you from debt when income stops.

Compound growth

Returns that earn returns. Saving AED 1,000 monthly at a reasonable rate beats one big deposit made ten years from now — by a wide margin.

Diversification

Never put all your money in one asset. Spreading risk across different channels limits a big loss even if one channel gets hit.

Liquidity

How fast an asset turns into spendable cash without losing value. Money you may need soon should stay in fast, liquid places.

Opportunity cost

Every dirham spent on something unnecessary loses the return it could have earned. Ask: is this purchase worth what it gives up?

Return vs. risk

Higher return always means higher risk. Any "guaranteed" high return is a red flag — not an opportunity.

The UAE market

Where should your money go? Local options

Low risk

Deposits & high-yield savings accounts

Offered by banks licensed by the UAE Central Bank. Guaranteed, low-risk returns — the right home for your emergency fund.

Medium risk

Mutual funds & national sukuk

Funds licensed by the Securities and Commodities Authority, plus Sharia-compliant sukuk — a friendly entry point for beginners.

Higher risk

Local shares (Dubai / Abu Dhabi markets)

Direct investment in listed companies. It needs diversification and a long horizon — avoid short-term speculation.

Before you start

  • Begin with an amount you can afford to lose, and spread your entries over stages (dollar-cost averaging).
  • Verify the provider is licensed by the regulator or the Central Bank before transferring any money.
  • Never hand your money to someone acting on your behalf without a written, licensed agreement.
